NextX Agency Financial Systems Audit 12 August 2026

Build the ledger. Don't migrate to Odoo.

A review of the money-handling layer across 47 data models, 53 API routes and every client-side financial write path — reconciled against the live production database, and assessed for what it takes to run several legal entities on it.

27 findings 6 critical 10 high 10 medium 1 low Est. remediation 13–17 weeks

The decision

Adopt Odoo's accounting model, not Odoo itself.

The instinct is right — Odoo 19's automated bill-processing flow is genuinely good, and real accounting is what you're missing. But your system is not primarily an accounting system. It's an operational system with accounting attached, and the operational half is where your business lives: per-person cash wallets in SRD and USD, per-category seller commissions, location-scoped stock and reservations, dual catalogs, the webshop and CMS.

Odoo replaces maybe 30% of that and forces a rewrite of the rest into Python, XML and OWL — 4–8 months, Enterprise licensing per user per company, and no operational features shipping throughout. Meanwhile you have already built the part most teams get wrong. finance_ledger_entries is append-only and enforced in the database by prevent_finance_ledger_mutation(), with automatic capture from wallet transactions via trigger — and production confirms it works: 490 wallet transactions, 490 ledger entries, zero gaps. What's missing on top of it — a chart of accounts and balanced debit/credit legs — is weeks of work, not months.

One thing worth naming: a company entity has to be built either way. Odoo wouldn't spare you the modelling work of deciding what a legal entity means in your business. It would only move that work into an unfamiliar stack.

A caveat, stated plainly: the reconciliation in §3 means your books would not survive an external audit today. Roughly 6.7% of recorded revenue carries no cost of goods, and SRD 9,458 of commission payouts never reached the expense ledger at all. That is an argument for fixing the write paths urgently — not for migrating. Moving to Odoo on top of unreliable data relocates the problem, and you would still have to clean it before import.

§ 01

What Odoo trades away

What you'd gain

Double-entry general ledger, chart of accounts, native multi-company with consolidation, multi-currency with FX revaluation, AP/AR aging, bank reconciliation, tax and VAT reporting, lock dates, and the automated bill ingestion you're inspired by.

What you'd give up

The operational layer that is roughly 70% of the system and all of your edge — plus the Next.js and TypeScript stack your team is already productive in.

When to revisit this

If you need statutory audited financials or VAT filings across several legal entities, add Odoo — or any standard package — downstream as the book of record, fed by a nightly journal export from your own ledger. You get compliance and BPA-style automation without surrendering the operational system. Adopt it as a consumer of your data, not a replacement for it.

§ 02

What's already right

Worth stating plainly, because it's the reason the build path works at all.

  • Immutable ledger. finance_ledger_entries blocks UPDATE and DELETE at the database layer, with a message directing you to corrective entries. This is correct practice.
  • Automatic ledger capture, verified. The wallet_transactions_capture_ledger trigger has captured 490 of 490 wallet transactions with no gaps — the backstop holds despite finding F-02.
  • FX snapshot on sales, verified. Zero USD sales are missing an exchange rate. That discipline has held across every sale ever recorded.
  • Commission arithmetic is internally consistent. Zero rows where the commission amount disagrees with rate × sale total. The maths is right; the plumbing around it is where the problems are.
  • No negative stock, no negative wallet balances.
  • Correlation IDs on every ledger entry — the foundation for grouping the legs of a future double-entry transaction.
  • Expense control columns exist — classification, status, vendor, receipt, reviewer, refunds. The schema thinking is right; they're simply unused (F-20).
  • DECIMAL throughout rather than floating point, and an obligations table with due dates and source tracing.

§ 04

Findings

F-01Critical

Historical gross profit is retroactively mutable

sale_items records unit_price but no cost. COGS is computed from live master data in reportCalculations.ts:107, reading item.purchasePriceUsd at report time.

Editing an item's purchase price silently rewrites gross profit on every past sale of that item — last quarter's margins change when you renegotiate with a supplier. Compounding it, cogsSrd = cogsUsd × exchangeRate applies a current rate to a historical cost, so SRD margins drift with every FX move.

FixAdd unit_cost_usd and fx_rate_at_sale to sale_items, written at sale time. Backfill from current values, flagged as estimated.

F-02Critical

Financial writes are client-side, non-atomic, on the anon key

sales/page.tsx:467–860 performs roughly eight sequential unguarded writes from the browser: sales → sale_items → stock → commissions → wallets → wallet_transactions. Any failure or closed tab mid-sequence leaves partial financial state.

The only rollback is a manual delete of the sale header at line 509 — which does not undo stock decrements or wallet credits that happen after it. The existence of create-missing-commissions, recalculate-commissions, check-commission, fix-combo-price and a root-level check-commission-currency.js is direct evidence this path drifts in production.

FixOne server route, one prisma.$transaction. The pattern already exists at api/seller/sales/route.ts:89.

F-03Critical

Wallet and stock balances use read-modify-write

sales/page.tsx:715 writes balance: matchingWallet.balance + total, where the balance was read at page load. Two concurrent sales against the same wallet means the second overwrites the first — and that money vanishes from the balance with no error. Same pattern for stock at line 528.

FixAtomic SQL increments, or derive balances from the ledger and treat wallets.balance as a cache.

F-04Critical

Every authenticated user has full control of all financial data

All 80 RLS policies follow one shape: FOR ALL USING (auth.role() = 'authenticated') — on wallets, expenses, sales, commissions and exchange rates, with no location scoping and no role check.

Combined with the anon key shipped to the browser, any user who can log in can rewrite any wallet balance or delete any commission from the browser console. There is no segregation of duties at the data layer, and this gets materially worse the moment two legal entities share a database.

F-05Critical

Financial mutation endpoints are gated only by an unvalidated cookie

CorrectedThe first draft of this audit said these endpoints had no guard at all. They do have one — src/proxy.ts, Next 16's middleware — but it does not do what it looks like it does.

/api/delete-commissions, /api/recalculate-commissions, /api/fix-combo-price, /api/migrate and /api/delete have no requireAdmin call in their handlers (compare /api/debug-profit, which does). They are matched by the proxy, which rejects requests carrying no nextics_session cookie.

But the proxy only checks the cookie exists. It never validates it — its own comment says the session is "opaque and verified in route handlers," and these handlers do no verification. So any forged value passes (document.cookie = 'nextics_session=x'), and there is no role check, so a seller account passes too.

Net exposure is unchanged for /api/delete-commissions, which hard-deletes commission rows by ID: one unauthenticated request destroys commission history. The guard is real but decorative.

FixAdd requireAdmin to each handler, or delete them. Repair endpoints shouldn't exist in production once F-02 is fixed.

F-15Critical

Commission payouts write to columns that don't exist, and fail silently

commissions/page.tsx:343 inserts category, payment_method and date into the expenses table. None of those columns exist — and the table's wallet_id is NOT NULL but is never supplied. The insert fails every single time, and the result is never checked: no error destructuring, no .select(). It fails without a sound.

Production evidence: zero commission-payout expenses exist, against 106 commissions marked paid worth SRD 9,458.05. Operating expenses are understated by that amount, so every margin and profit figure the dashboard has ever shown is correspondingly overstated.

FixCorrect the column names, supply wallet_id, check the error, and move the payout into the same server-side transaction that flips commissions.paid.

F-06High

"Undo Sale" hard-deletes financial records

The undo path deletes commissions, then sale items, then the sale. Accounting convention is never to delete — you post a reversing entry. Because the ledger is correctly immutable while the subledger rows are destroyed, ledger and operational tables permanently diverge after every undo.

FixA voided status plus contra entries sharing the original correlation_id.

F-07High

No company or legal entity in the data model

The schema has Location and nothing above it; every financial table scopes by location_id. A location is not a legal entity — one company has many locations, and multi-company demands per-entity books, intercompany elimination and separate statements. This is the central blocker for the direction you're heading.

F-08High

The ledger is single-entry

finance_ledger_entries has a direction of in or out and a single amount. That's a cash-movement journal, not double-entry. Without a chart of accounts and balanced legs you can produce a cash-flow view but not a balance sheet or a trial balance — and no way to prove the books balance. Inventory, receivables, payables and equity are invisible as accounts.

F-09High

Decimal(10,2) will overflow

26 money columns cap at 99,999,999.99. At 38 SRD per USD that ceiling is about USD 2.6M. Your largest single wallet holds SRD 18,255.99 and lifetime revenue is SRD 211,976 — comfortable now, but cumulative aggregates and multi-entity consolidation shorten the runway fast. Rates at Decimal(10,4) are tight too.

FixDecimal(18,4) for amounts, Decimal(18,8) for rates.

F-16High

Commissions marked paid without a matching wallet debit

SRD 9,458.05 of commissions are flagged paid, but commission-related wallet debits total only SRD 8,006.67 across 11 transactions. SRD 1,451.38 was marked as paid without money leaving any wallet on record. Either cash left the business untracked, or the flag was set without payment. Both need reconciling by hand before any ledger migration.

F-17High

Invoice numbers are random, never stored, and not reproducible

sales/page.tsx:433 builds INV-YYYYMMDD-{random} from Math.random(). There is no invoice_number column anywhere in the schema, so the number handed to the customer exists only in that browser session and the activity log.

Reprinting later calls a different function (line 949) that builds INV-YYYYMMDD-{last 6 of the UUID} — a different number than the customer received. Reservations add a third format, RES-COMP-{timestamp}.

So you cannot match a customer's invoice to a sale record; numbering is neither sequential nor gapless, which most jurisdictions require for tax; and random four-digit suffixes collide within a day at a meaningful rate.

FixA persisted, per-company, gapless sequence allocated inside the sale transaction and stored on the sale.

F-18High

6.7% of recorded revenue carries no cost of goods

SRD 14,220.02 of SRD 211,976.02 — SRD 11,420.02 from the four item-less headers, SRD 2,800.00 from five active items priced at zero cost. All of it currently reports at 100% margin. This is separate from, and additive to, the F-01 distortion.

F-10Medium

No FX revaluation or gain/loss tracking

The USD 534.00 held across your wallets is never revalued, and there is no FX gain/loss account. Under SRD volatility this misstates results by a material amount.

F-19Medium

The exchange rate is 68 days stale

One active rate, fixed at 38.0000, last set 2026-06-05. Every USD-to-SRD conversion in reporting — and the snapshot written onto every new sale — uses a rate over two months old. This compounds the cogsSrd problem in F-01.

F-11Medium

No period close or lock date

Nothing prevents backdating into a reported month or editing last year. Every serious system has a lock date and a close checklist behind it.

F-12Medium

Commission isn't recognised as a liability when earned

It's inserted client-side alongside the sale, then becomes a wallet movement only when someone manually pays it. 16 unpaid commissions worth SRD 1,431.63 sit as an unrecorded obligation today — so margins look better than they are.

F-13Medium

Obligations are disconnected from the ledger

finance_obligations tracks receivables and payables in its own table with no AR/AP accounts behind it, so it can't roll into a balance sheet or produce an aging report.

F-14Medium

Budget spend is an unreconciled counter

budgets.amount_spent is denormalised with no visible reconciliation against actual expense records. Only one budget exists.

F-20Medium

Expense controls are 100% unused

All 83 expenses lack a date, a vendor, a receipt number and a review, and all 83 are unclassified. The migration shipped columns nobody fills, so reporting expenses by category is impossible today. Controls have to be required at the point of entry, not merely available.

F-21Medium

No wallet has ever been reconciled

Zero rows in wallet_reconciliations, against SRD 42,005.99 and USD 534.00 held across 13 wallets. For a cash-heavy business this is the largest theft-and-error exposure you have — and it's the control most likely to have caught the SRD 500 drift in F-03.

F-22Medium

The ledger-guard functions are the least hardened objects in the schema

capture_wallet_transaction_ledger() is SECURITY DEFINER and remains executable by the anon and authenticated roles over /rest/v1/rpc/ — the migration's REVOKE ... FROM PUBLIC doesn't cover explicit role grants. And prevent_finance_ledger_mutation(), the function enforcing ledger immutability itself, has a mutable search_path.

FixREVOKE EXECUTE ... FROM anon, authenticated and add SET search_path = public to both.

F-24High

No recurring expense mechanism exists, so subscriptions are invisible

No table in the database models a schedule — nothing matching recurring, subscription, payout or schedule. Every expense is entered by hand, one at a time.

Production evidence: across 83 expenses since January, exactly one mentions a subscription vendor. Your expense categories are Business Expense · Copilot · Marketing · Personal Items · Shipping — no software or subscription category, and that lone Copilot category suggests one tool was tracked once and the habit lapsed.

So Codex, Claude and Spotify have been charging you for months while the books show almost nothing. Operating costs are understated by their full run rate, and because no wallet is ever debited, cash on hand in the system drifts above reality.

§ 05

Target architecture

Four additions on top of what already exists.

1 — Company, the legal entity

Company carries name, legal name, base currency, tax ID and fiscal year start; Location.company_id points at it. Every financial table gains company_id, denormalised deliberately so RLS and reporting never need a join to enforce isolation. Backfill existing rows to a default company — the migration is mechanical.

2 — Account, a chart of accounts per company

1000  Assets       1010 Cash SRD · 1020 Cash USD · 1200 Receivable · 1400 Inventory
2000  Liabilities  2100 Payable · 2200 Commissions Payable · 2300 Tax Payable
3000  Equity       3100 Owner Capital · 3200 Retained Earnings · 3300 Owner Draws
4000  Income       4100 Product Sales · 4900 FX Gain
5000  Expenses     5000 COGS · 5100 Payroll · 5200 Marketing · 5900 FX Loss

Each wallet maps to an account; each expense classification maps to an account. That mapping is most of the migration.

3 — Journal entries, double-entry above the existing ledger

JournalEntry holds company, date, correlation ID, source, posting metadata and a reversal pointer. JournalLine holds account, debit, credit, currency, FX rate, base amount, location and seller. Enforce that debits equal credits per entry with a trigger — the same approach already proven on finance_ledger_entries. Keep that table as the cash subledger; the journal becomes the book of record above it. A sale then posts as one balanced entry: 

Dr 1010 Cash SRD              5,600.00   — wallet receives
Dr 5000 COGS                  3,200.00   — snapshotted cost, fixes F-01
   Cr 4100 Product Sales                 5,600.00
   Cr 1400 Inventory                     3,200.00
Dr 5300 Commission Expense      280.00
   Cr 2200 Commissions Payable             280.00   — fixes F-12

4 — Accounting period, the lock

A period per company with an open, closed or locked status. A trigger rejects any journal line dated inside a locked period — which fixes F-11 and makes F-01 structurally impossible going forward.

Intercompany

When company A pays an expense for company B, post two balanced entries linked by correlation_id, hitting Due from Related Party and Due to Related Party. Consolidated reporting sums all entities and eliminates those two accounts against each other.

§ 06

Specification: the three gaps

Written to be built in one pass, on top of Phase 1. The existing POST /api/expenses handler is the reference for all money movement here — serializable transaction, markFinanceLedgerRecorded, explicit ledger entry, activity log inside the transaction. Nothing below should invent a new pattern.

6.1 — Subscriptions that deduct themselves

One new table, recurring_expenses: name and vendor, amount and currency, cadence (weekly / monthly / quarterly / yearly), an anchor day clamped to short months so the 31st bills on Feb 28 rather than rolling into March, the wallet to debit, location, category, classification, next_run_on, and active / auto-post flags. Plus two columns on expenses: recurring_expense_id and period_key.

The idempotency rule is the part that must not be got wrong. A period key identifies the billing period — 2026-08, 2026-W33, 2026-Q3, 2026. A partial unique index on (recurring_expense_id, period_key) makes a second charge for the same period impossible at the database level. A cron firing twice, a retry after timeout, someone pressing "run now" during the scheduled run — none can double-charge, because the second insert violates the index rather than relying on application logic to notice.

A daily Vercel cron posts what's due. Four rules carry the weight:

  • Catch-up caps at 3 periods per run. If the cron is down four months, a subscription must not fire four charges at once and drain the wallet.
  • Insufficient balance skips, never fails. Leave next_run_on untouched so it retries, notify admins once per period, and keep processing the others. One short wallet must never block every subscription.
  • New schedules never back-charge. next_run_on is the next upcoming occurrence, so adding Spotify today doesn't invent seven months of history.
  • Each schedule posts in its own currency — no implicit FX at posting time.

Add the new API paths to PROTECTED_API_PREFIXES in src/proxy.ts and call requireAdmin in each handler — F-05 is exactly what happens when only the first is done. Then add a Software & subscriptions category, create Codex, Claude and Spotify against the USD wallet with their real amounts and renewal days, and retire the stray Copilot category into it.

Done meansEach subscription posts exactly one expense per period with a matching wallet debit and ledger entry; running the job twice produces no second charge; a short wallet notifies instead of failing silently; and /finance shows the next 30 days of scheduled charges.

6.2 — A signal for "too much stock"

No schema change. This is a read model computed from stock, sale items and item cost.

dailyVelocity  = unitsSoldInWindow / 90
daysOfCover    = quantityOnHand / dailyVelocity      — undefined when velocity is 0
cashTiedUpUsd  = quantityOnHand × purchasePriceUsd

Status, in order: out when nothing is on hand, dead when velocity is zero but stock is not, overstocked above 120 days of cover, low below 14 days, otherwise healthy.

Excess is not the same as cash tied up, and conflating them overstates the problem. Dead stock is entirely excess. For overstock only the tail beyond target cover counts — a fast seller holding depth is doing the right thing: 

excessUnits = quantityOnHand − ceil(dailyVelocity × 120)

Releasable cash is dead plus excess: the money you'd get back by clearing it. Report zero-cost items separately rather than counting them as free stock. Lead the panel with releasable cash, then a table sorted by excess capital — worst offender first, because that's the reorder decision. Thresholds must be adjustable; 120 and 14 days are defaults, not truths about your business.

Done meansThe figures reconcile against F-25 — USD 503.02 dead, USD 177.90 overstocked, USD 680.92 releasable; selling a dead item moves it out of dead on next load; thresholds change without a deploy.

6.3 — Paying yourselves, traceably

No new table. A draw is an expense with classification = 'owner_draw', which already exists, is already excluded from operating profit, and already flows through the ledger. Traceability comes free by reusing the expense path — that is the entire point. Two settings hold the policy: a reserve floor per currency. 

  cashOnHand            operational wallets, that currency
− unpaidCommissions     money already owed to sellers
− openPayables          obligations due within 30 days
− scheduledCharges      recurring expenses due in the next 30 days
− reserveFloor          3 × monthly operating cost
= safeToDraw

Computed per currency, never mixed across currencies. Show every line, not just the total — the value of this screen is that it explains itself, and a number with no derivation gets ignored the first time it looks wrong.

Guardrails: refuse a draw above the safe amount unless an admin overrides with a typed reason recorded on the expense; refuse entirely if the wallet would go negative; warn when the exchange rate is stale, since a USD draw priced off a 68-day-old rate misstates what left the business.

And make it a policy, not just a button. The convention here is a modest fixed monthly draw reviewed quarterly — not a sweep of whatever is spare. With revenue trending down and June at SRD 10,500, a fixed draw sized off the trailing three months is the right shape. Set the first amount only after Phase 1, when the run rate is real.

One caveat

Build this last of the three. The safe-to-draw number is only as honest as the expense classification behind it, and today every expense is unclassified — so the reserve floor would be computed from a run rate that mixes stock purchases with rent. Shipping this before F-20 and F-15 are fixed produces a confident, precise, wrong number, which is worse than no number at all.

  • Month-end checklist gating the period lock

Roughly 13–17 weeks on your existing stack, delivered incrementally — against 4–8 months of migration during which nothing else ships.

§ 08

Metrics to run the business on

Once Phase 3 lands these become computable per company and consolidated. Track them monthly.

Margin

  • Gross margin by item, category, location and company — trustworthy only after F-01
  • Contribution margin after commission and location costs: the number that says whether a location deserves more inventory

Cash

  • Net burn and runway in months
  • Cash conversion cycle: DIO + DSO − DPO
  • Inventory turns and dead stock past 90 days — where cash hides in an inventory business

Growth

  • Month-over-month revenue, with same-location growth separated from new-location growth
  • Revenue per location and per seller
  • Average order value and units per transaction

Working capital

  • Receivables aging at 30, 60 and 90 days
  • Payables aging and upcoming obligations against projected cash

Discipline

  • Discount leakage: custom-price sales against list, by seller — already captured, nothing reports on it
  • Expense ratio by classification against revenue — impossible today at 100% unclassified
  • Wallet reconciliation coverage: % reconciled this month. Currently 0%
  • Budget variance, actual against plan

The close

  • Monthly close by working day five: reconcile every wallet, review unclassified expenses, revalue FX, lock the period, publish the pack
  • The habit is worth as much as the software
